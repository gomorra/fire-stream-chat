scripts/run-plan.sh

How the plan runner gets a plan from step 1 to merged

The runner is a bash loop with no model in it. It starts one fresh headless Claude session per step, believes nothing that session says until it has checked the branch itself, and stops for you in exactly three situations. Everything it knows between runs is written in the plan file.

Who writes what

The loop over the plan

The plan's Order: line is the program. The driver walks its tokens left to right; ‖ is a checkpoint, a number is a step.

One step, including everything that can go wrong

A step gets at most three kinds of help, each once per step, even across re-runs of the driver: a nudge, then a fresh attempt one effort rung higher, then you.

When it stops, and what it wants from you

ExitMeansWhat you do
0Every step is shipped on the plan branch.Read report.sh <run-id> and the departures in the Shipped blocks, then merge --ff-only and push.
2A session hit something that would change a decision or the design of the plan. It wrote a Decision needed block and committed nothing.Resume the printed session and answer, or rename the block to Decision taken in the branch's plan. Run again.
3Blocked: budget spent, no usable result, an environment or spec problem, or still failing after the nudge and the escalated attempt.Look at the worktree. Resume the session or fix by hand, then run again; a step you finished by hand is validated before anything else.
4A ‖ checkpoint after a step this run shipped.Sign off the departures. Run again to continue.
1Usage or configuration error, or a bug in the driver itself.Read the message. Nothing was launched, or the trap names the line.

What it checks, and what it takes on a session's word

Checked by the driver

  • That commits exist and the Shipped line points at one of them.
  • That the gate is green, by running it again in the worktree.
  • That the diff's size and paths got the review skills they require.
  • How the @Test count moved, and which test files were deleted.
  • That a hand-finished step is judged on its own Shipped line and last attempt, never on what a discarded attempt claimed.
  • That a variant file holds only known keys and sane values, before anything is launched.

Taken on trust

  • That a skill listed as run was really run.
  • That the step met its spec. The gate only shows nothing regressed; the judge grades this from a throwaway checkout, but nothing acts on the grade yet.
  • That a departure recorded for sign-off was a reasonable call. That is what the checkpoint and your read of the Shipped blocks are for.

Where a variant comes in

--variant b loads scripts/plan-runner/variants/b.env over the driver's tunables, which decide only three things per tier: model, effort, advisor. The same file can switch the judge on and escalation off. Everything the run produces is then named after <plan>-b: branch, worktree, log and result files, so two configurations of one plan sit side by side and report.sh can print them together. A step's own tags still win: model: strong keeps a step on the strong tier under every variant.
